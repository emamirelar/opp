| Opportunity Substage:Decide | The Go Decision point (Decide stage) | Opportunity Identification culminates in a decision \- the Opportunity Manager will send the Opportunity for formal consideration to the decision maker, who will be responsible for indicating the requirements to proceed (Go) or decide not to pursue the opportunity further (No-Go) | Goal To determine whether to commit UNOPS resources to further developing the opportunity, based on its alignment with strategic priorities, initial feasibility, and value to partners. This sub-stage is the first formal decision control point in the Opportunity Stage. Based on the profiling analysis and draft documentation, the DOA holder reviews the opportunity and determines whether it should proceed to full development. A “Go” decision authorises the use of resources and personnel to refine the opportunity into a final concept note and legal package. A “No-Go” decision archives the opportunity or places it on hold. In either case, the decision must be documented, justified, and auditable. | Engagements go through multiple stages in oUP. This will essentially replace parts of Opportunity & Pre-engagement stages (initiation is no longer relevant as the project will exist from the outset). There is currently no requirement to justify cancelling an opportunity. | Business requirements A DOA holder must be able to review the full opportunity package, including: Final draft of the Opportunity Statement, including DST insights and early findings; Draft Concept Note, as appropriate for partner engagement; Draft Opportunity Development Plan, including budget and personnel requirements; Any due diligence status flags or open issues. A DOA holder must be able to provide comments and direction to the opportunity team, which must be documented in the final Opportunity Statement. This direction forms the basis for downstream development efforts. An Opportunity Manager must be able to finalise the Opportunity Statement, incorporating: Direction from the DOA holder; Updated rationale for the opportunity; Summary of profiling insights and partner engagement to date; An outline of anticipated next steps, if approved. An Opportunity Manager must be able to finalise the Concept Note, tailoring the format to the partner’s expectations (UNOPS or partner-specific), so that the opportunity is ready for external validation and negotiation. A DOA holder must be able to make and record a Go / No-Go decision in the system, including: Selection of the decision outcome (Go / No-Go); Timestamp of decision; Name and DOA level of the decision-maker; Decision rationale (linked to the Opportunity Statement), noting any key risks and opportunities, and linking to the draft risk register; Any conditions, directions, or caveats applied to the Go decision. A Go decision must authorise the use of the defined opportunity-stage budget and personnel, as specified in the Opportunity Development Plan, so that development work can begin immediately. The system must store and version control the finalised Opportunity Statement, so that institutional records reflect the decision history and rationale. A DOA holder must be able to delegate the decision-making responsibility, in line with the DOA Master Table and regional configuration. The mechanism for such delegation must be auditable and reflected in the opportunity record. A DOA holder must be able to configure a checklist of required documentation or inputs, which must be completed before an opportunity is submitted for decision. These requirements must be visible to the Opportunity Manager and cascade down to subordinate DOA levels. Success criteria Decision is documented, justified, and aligned with delegated authority Final Opportunity Statement and Concept Note are finalised Development team, scope, and budget are confirmed DOA direction to the development team is recorded Opportunity record is updated to reflect Go / No-Go status Inputs DST profile and insights Finalised Opportunity Statement Draft Concept Note Draft Opportunity Development Plan (personnel, work plan, and budget) Due diligence data and risk considerations Partner engagement history and context Outputs Initiative structure confirmed (Project, Programme, Portfolio) Finalised Opportunity Statement with DOA direction Finalised Concept Note Documented Go / No-Go decision Updated opportunity record and system status Authorised team, budget, and plan for development (if Go) Primary stakeholders DOA holder (default: DOA3, per the DOA Master Table and regional configuration) Opportunity Manager Partnerships specialist Technical and policy advisors (e.g. legal, infrastructure, finance, procurement) Regional leadership team (as needed based on escalation or complexity) |
